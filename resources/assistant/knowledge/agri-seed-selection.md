Quality seed should be true to its intended variety, clean, viable and vigorous. Seed quality affects crop establishment; a variety name alone does not prove a seed lot is good.

1. Choose a locally suitable variety and a reliable seed source.
2. Check seed identity, lot information, physical condition and available germination information.
3. Protect seed from moisture, pests and damage during handling and storage.
4. Follow locally appropriate sowing guidance and evaluate emergence.

Seed needs proper production, harvesting and processing to preserve viability. Do not promise a yield or invent a certified supplier. Variety, seed rate, depth and calendar need crop-specific local advice. A system seed-release record is not evidence that seed was planted or that it germinated.
