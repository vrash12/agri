Integrated pest management combines observation, prevention and suitable control methods to keep crop damage at an acceptable level while limiting risks from pesticides. Not every insect is a pest; natural enemies can help protect crops.

1. Inspect the crop and note the affected plant parts and distribution of damage.
2. Identify the organism or problem with an agriculture technician before selecting a control.
3. Consider appropriate crop management and nonchemical measures while protecting beneficial organisms.
4. Monitor the result and obtain locally approved advice if control is needed.

Yellow leaves or poor growth alone do not establish a pest, disease or nutrient deficiency. This chat cannot diagnose a field. It does not prescribe a pesticide, mixture, dose or spraying schedule. Any pesticide use must follow its approved label and competent local guidance.
