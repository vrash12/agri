Crop rotation grows different crops in sequence on the same land across seasons. Intercropping grows different crops together during the same period. They are distinct ways to diversify production.

A well-planned rotation can vary rooting patterns and interrupt some pest cycles. Suitable cover crops can protect soil between main crops. These benefits depend on the species and local management; a rotation does not eliminate all pests or fertilizer needs.

1. Review the previous crop and known field constraints.
2. Compare locally suitable crops and their water, nutrient and labour requirements.
3. Plan the sequence and soil cover with local extension guidance.
4. Keep farm observations to assess the result.

Do not prescribe a fixed rotation calendar without local information.
