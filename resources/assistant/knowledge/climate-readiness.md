El Niño readiness and climate evidence

Open El Niño Readiness in the office menu. Choose an authorized municipality and select Show area. Open risk map shows municipality/barangay planning outlines; tables remain usable without the map.

Normal means routine monitoring; Watch means verify dry conditions; Alert means prepare local response; Critical means prioritize field response. These are office priorities, not PAGASA ENSO alerts. The municipality uses its highest current area assessment. Gray or Not assessed means no current assessment, not that conditions are safe. Barangays need their own assessment; municipality colour does not mark every parcel affected.

To record one, authorized provincial agriculture staff or Municipal Head/Staff select Record an assessment. Choose the municipality and optional barangay; enter the priority, source drought/rainfall categories, observation date and review-by date within 31 days. Add a field-review reference or a direct HTTPS PAGASA bulletin link, verify its area/date, optionally select reported affected parcels, then Save assessment. System Owner, Regional Head and Super Admin have read-only oversight.

Zero counts can mean no matching current assessments or no explicitly linked parcels. Check area/level filters and dates. Do not enter invented conditions just to fill the dashboard.

Automated evidence shows dated Open-Meteo forecast snapshots and delayed CHIRPS rainfall/reference snapshots. They support review; they never set office levels, declare drought or link affected parcels. Load forecast uses the municipality reference point, not each farm. The scheduled refresh needs the administrator's scheduler; unavailable evidence is not zero rainfall.

Saved parcel NDVI checks show observation dates and usable pixels. Satellite checks are on-demand by default; NDMI, SAVI and EVI do not populate the NDVI summary. Crop stage, cloud cover, rainfall, irrigation and field inspection matter. For a parcel, open Satellite field check. Use official PAGASA sources for declarations.

The guide can explain this workflow but cannot inspect current climate records, fetch fresh evidence, save assessments or decide whether a particular farm is damaged or safe.
