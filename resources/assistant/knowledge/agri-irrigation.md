Irrigation supplies water to crops when rainfall and stored soil water are insufficient. Effective water management considers crop stage, soil conditions, rainfall, drainage and reliable water supply. Water shortage and excess water can both cause problems; a fixed daily watering schedule is not suitable for every crop or field.

1. Review crop needs and current field moisture.
2. Inspect water delivery and drainage and repair avoidable losses.
3. Use locally appropriate scheduling and observe the crop response.
4. Check official weather information when planning field work.

IRRI describes rice water-saving approaches such as alternate wetting and drying. These need suitable field conditions and local guidance; do not transfer a rice flooding schedule to corn or vegetables. This chat has not measured soil moisture or checked current rainfall and cannot declare a drought level.
